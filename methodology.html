THE PRELIMINARY CONCLUSION

What our source-led analysis suggests

The sampled documents show continuing official partnership prescriptions alongside academic and media attention to strategic competition, India's autonomous calculations and persistent border tensions. The coexistence of these narratives is visible before and after 2020, and following the diplomatic re-engagement of 2024–2026.

Supporting examples: 2019 Fudan study ↗; 2021 MFA readout ↗; 2024 academic commentary ↗.

This is a preliminary interpretation of selected sources; our current pilot cannot demonstrate representative changes in framing over time.
01 / SCOPE

What this project measures

The tracker identifies how selected Chinese institutions and commentators represent India's strategic role and geopolitical choices between 2019 and 2026. The unit shown here is one reviewed, source-attributed claim record per distinct source document. More than one role may coexist in the original document, even when a record highlights a primary research frame.

The pilot draws on a historical working inventory of source leads and a later researcher-led review queue. It is not an exhaustive corpus or probability sample.

Do not interpret record counts as the share of Chinese officials, scholars, media institutions or the public holding a particular view.
02 / SOURCE TYPES

Whose view is being coded?

Government & CCP

Statements attributable to officials, ministries or party organs. A minister's statement quoted by a newspaper remains an official statement.

Academia & think tanks

Research articles, institutional studies and attributed scholarly commentary. Publication through a newspaper does not automatically make the claim an editorial stance.

State media

Editorials and signed commentaries expressing the publication's or author-commentator's framing. Ordinary news reports quoting experts are attributed carefully.

Not yet quantitatively represented: business/industry and social-media discourse. These require their own sampling criteria and sufficient source coverage.

03 / CODING FRAMEWORK

What is recorded?

Topic

Border security, bilateral diplomacy, strategic status, US–India relations, regional order, economics, BRICS and Global South.

Primary framing

Cooperation, competition, mixed, conditional cooperation, security concern and references to strategic autonomy. These are descriptive research labels, not an ideological or moral rating.

Provenance

Exact source URL, publication host, attributed voice, document type, source-language anchor and a short analytical paraphrase.

Qualifications

Date precision, republished material, access level and attribution caveats remain visible at the record level.

04 / QUALITY

What has—and has not—been validated?

The records displayed here were selected from a researcher-led, source-linked review queue. They have not been independently double-coded. Earlier source leads and first-pass claims that were not included in this review are intentionally absent from the public starter dataset.

Where a source is a repost, editorial, interview or academic interpretation, its genre is recorded. Month-only dates and attribution problems are identified rather than silently standardised.

Annual coverage is uneven. The dashboard therefore charts the size and composition of the selected sample, not a longitudinal change in Chinese discourse prevalence. It cannot establish that Galwan or any summit caused a change in a source's framing.

This prototype is suitable for research exploration and testing the website. A public scholarly release would require systematic search documentation, fuller evidence verification, updated sampling and internal adjudication of ambiguous codes.
05 / DATA AND UPDATES

Open, traceable records

This static website reads the file data/records.json and offers data/records.csv for download. The original sources remain on their publishers' websites and can change or disappear. The current pilot reflects the research review dated 29 October 2026.

To update the public tracker, edit the JSON and CSV files in the GitHub repository, review new records against the same codebook, and commit the changes. GitHub Pages will then serve the revised files.
